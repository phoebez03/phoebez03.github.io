01 · Problem

Students were preparing for the test.
Not learning to reason.

Pennsylvania's Keystone Biology exam has a persistently low passing rate, with an even wider gap in Title I schools. With no dedicated preparation tool, students relied on generic practice that rewarded memorization while teachers lacked visibility into where reasoning broke down.

01

No Keystone-specific tool

Students lacked practice designed around the exam's real format and standards.

02

Memorization over reasoning

Students learned to pattern-match answers instead of explaining biological concepts.

03

No teacher visibility

Teachers could see scores, but not misconceptions or who needed intervention.

02 · Process

One continuous learning loop.

MVP 01

Understand & Scaffold

Started with the learning problem. I researched where students struggled, designed multiple scaffolding modes, then simplified them when testing revealed over-support and friction.

Decision 01

Design the level of support, not just the interface

Product Thinking · Iteration · Tradeoffs

Context
For MVP 1, I designed four practice modes—Guided, Practice, Exam, and Review—with different levels of scaffolding for different learning stages.
Problem
Testing showed students keyword-matching answer choices instead of reasoning. Guided Mode felt too easy, while Practice Mode felt long and repetitive.
Decision
I merged Guided and Practice into one experience with on-demand hints and conditional support, so help appeared when needed without forcing every student through the same scaffolding.
Product screenPractice and exam mode selection
Decision 02

Design features around behavior, not assumptions

User Research · Feature Design · Iteration

Context
For short-answer questions, we initially focused on helping students answer directly inside the practice flow.
Problem
Testing revealed that students sometimes understood individual concepts but struggled to organize them into a complete response.
Decision
We introduced free-form notes and guided prompts, giving students space to externalize and structure their thinking before answering.
Interaction walkthroughShort-answer practice flow
Decision 03

Design dashboards around decisions, not data

Information Architecture · Systems Thinking · Visual Hierarchy

Context
Teachers needed to understand progress, mastery, completion, areas of difficulty, and individual performance.
Problem
My early dashboard surfaced too much at once. The challenge was not adding more data—it was deciding what teachers needed to see first.
Decision
I reorganized the dashboard around actionable signals, using hierarchy, filtering, and progressive disclosure to surface students needing attention while keeping deeper data accessible.

Decision 04

Design for implementation, not just handoff

Collaboration · Design-to-Dev · Prototyping

Context
Five MVP cycles meant new evidence continually changed features, interactions, and priorities.
Problem
Static handoffs made complex interaction logic difficult to communicate and slowed iteration between design and development.
Decision
I worked alongside developers, researchers, and the project lead throughout each cycle, using interactive and functional prototypes to clarify behavior, test earlier, and refine designs during implementation.

03 · Reflection

Begin with the problem.
Keep learning together.

This project taught me to ground decisions in research and testing, and to treat iteration as part of design—not a step after it. It also reinforced that strong products come from close collaboration: aligning research, product thinking, design, and development throughout the process.
