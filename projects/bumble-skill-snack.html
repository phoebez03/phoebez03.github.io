The concept

Designing a reason
to start a conversation.

Skill Snack is an interest-based conversation feature that gives matches a low-pressure reason to interact. I took it from concept to high-fidelity prototypes across iOS, Android, and Apple Watch while working inside Bumble's existing system and native platform conventions.

01 · Problem

The match happened.
The conversation didn't.

The issue was not necessarily match quality—it was activation. Users reached the hardest moment with no specific reason to speak, while a blank text field placed the entire social burden on them.

01

Matches stalled

People were successfully matching, but a meaningful portion never converted that match into a conversation.

02

The blank-box burden

A blank message field asked users to invent a relevant, confident opening with no structure or shared starting point.

03

One idea, three platforms

The feature needed to feel unmistakably Bumble while adapting to different interaction patterns on mobile and Apple Watch.

02 · Process

From architecture
to native interaction.

I moved from the underlying flow to the interaction model, then translated the feature across three platforms. Each phase reduced uncertainty before increasing fidelity.

Stage 01

Flow & architecture

Low fidelity · IA · User flows

I mapped the end-to-end experience before touching visuals. The pivotal question was where Skill Snack could live in Bumble’s existing architecture without feeling bolted on.

Stage 02

Interaction model

Mid fidelity · Prototyping · Tradeoffs

I compared swipe, select, and browse patterns. Browse won because it respected the user’s pace and made choosing a prompt feel intentional rather than gimmicky.

Stage 03

Three-platform system

High fidelity · Design system · Native patterns

I built the feature to full fidelity for iOS, Android, and Apple Watch. The Watch forced every element to justify its existence: three actions, zero cognitive clutter.

Key design decisions

Novel in purpose.
Familiar in use.

Decision 01

Keep the experience light, even as the system grows complex

Product Thinking · Information Architecture · Prototyping

Context
The feature introduced different paths and states across men and women, free and paid users, and three platforms.
Problem
Supporting every case risked making a simple conversation starter feel fragmented and difficult to understand.
Decision
I mapped flows and wireframes before high-fidelity UI, then used rapid prototypes to explore edge cases. Simplifying the architecture first kept complexity in the system—not in the user experience.
Interaction walkthroughA focused mobile path into conversation
Decision 02

Extend the design system instead of reinventing it

Design System · Visual Hierarchy · Cross-platform Craft

Context
Skill Snack needed a distinct purpose while still feeling immediately familiar inside Bumble.
Problem
Early, more expressive layouts made the feature look novel, but that novelty also introduced unnecessary learning and interaction friction.
Decision
I treated Bumble’s typography, color, hierarchy, spacing, and component behavior as the foundation, extending existing patterns only where the new behavior required it.

03 · Reflection

Think beyond the screen.
Design the ecosystem.

This project strengthened both my Figma craft and product thinking. I learned to introduce something new without ignoring the product around it: the problem framing, competitive landscape, user groups, architecture, design system, and platform behavior all shaped the interface.
